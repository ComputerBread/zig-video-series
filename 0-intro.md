# let's learn Zig, low level programming and Computer Architecture

Welcome to this ambitious series of video in which we will learn about the Zig
programming language, and to do so, we'll need to learn a lot about computers,
because Zig assumes that you already know quite a lot.
So, this is not just about Zig, but also about low level systems, computer architecture,
operating systems and more!
I want you to become strong programmers with solid foundations
and the ability to become independant, and figure out things by yourself.
I will try to teach you everything I know, the way I wished someone did it for
me!

These videos are aimed to everyone, wether you have years of experience, or
whether you've never programmed before. Unfortunately, I don't remember how it
feels to not know how to program, and what may be obvious to me, might not be
for you, so knowing at least one programming language may be helpful, but
it shouldn't be required.
If you're already an expert, don't worry, I will indicate the parts or videos
that you can skip!
Now, I am not going to dumb things down, I am not going to pretend like you are
babies, and I am not going to hold your hands!
We will study difficult topics, and you'll have to work a bit!
My goal is to teach as much as possible in the shortest amount of time!

A lot of books and tutorials, teach you about simple and unnecessary details
that you will rarely need, but skips very fundamentals ideas and topics, that
you should know about!
I want to skip the useless details that you can learn by yourself, and teach you
solid foundations that you can build upon!

Now, let's talk about Zig.
Zig isn't stable, it hasn't reach 1.0 and that means that the language
can have breaking changes. However, I believe that most of the things I am
going to teach you isn't going to change, and should work with the latest
released version. And I want you to assume that these videos are made using
whatever the latest version is. Currently it's 0.16, if a new version comes out
I will start using it, and will indicate it in the description. I will try to
keep the videos up to date, so if you stumble upon something that doesn't work
anymore, please tell me in the comments so I can fix it!!!

My goal is to make a series of videos, with a specific order that you should
follow! However I don't want to spend months if not years, preparing these
videos. Instead, I plan on making, and releasing one video at the time, but as
a consequence, I may publish these videos out of order. Either because I am not
sure about the correct order, or because I felt like working on a specific
subject instead of following a strict order! But I will make an ordered playlist,
I will number the videos and will maintain a document with the list of the videos
in the right order.

I use Linux on a machine with an x64 architecture. And that's the platform I
will use to teach. So some of the code and examples that I will show, will be
specific to this config. However the big ideas and concepts should be the same
on most major platforms, and you shouldn't have any trouble if you are using
Windows or MacOs! But if you feel like installing Linux, that would be great!
A VirtualMachine with linux, or even just the Windows Subsystem for Linux works
too!

About AI:

Zig has a strong no-AI policy, I would advice against using LLMs to write zig
code, especially if you want to share it publicly or ask questions in zig
communities!

Zig has a strong no-AI policy (try to find it and show it). And, I am not the
biggest AI user myself, but I think the current LLMs are absolutely mind-blowing
and are incredible pieces of technology that you should use to your own benefits.
However, when it comes to learning, I think we need to be more careful with our
use of AI, and I would advice against using AI to write code! I think it's
perfectly fine to use an LLM to ask clarifying questions, to discuss some ideas,
or even to help you find bugs in your code, because sometimes you make silly
mistakes and it's unnecessary to waste hours for no real benefit!
But when learning, you should do the work to build the neuronal connections
inside your brain, so don't outsource the thinking.
